Chordless

Privacy policy

Last updated 30 October 2026

Chordless (chordless.app) is a web app for arranging setlists of worship songs, with their lyrics and chords. This page explains what it does with your information.

In short

  • Your songs and setlists live on your device, and in your own Google Drive if you sign in.
  • Chordless has no database of its own. We do not keep your songs, setlists or Google data on any server.
  • We do not sell, share or use your data for advertising. There are no ads and no tracking across sites.
  • Chordless sends anonymous reports of how it runs, to find and fix problems. You can turn them off (see Anonymous usage reports).

What Chordless accesses

You can use Chordless without signing in. If you sign in with Google, it asks for:

  • Your name, email address and profile picture (the openid, email and profile scopes). They show who is signed in, and the email address names the leader of a setlist.
  • Google Drive access limited to files Chordless creates or you open with it (the drive.file scope). Chordless creates a "Chordless" folder in your Drive and keeps your songs and setlists there, so they sync between your devices and are backed up. It cannot see any other file in your Drive. If you share that folder with other people, it can also list who has access, so you can pick a setlist leader from them.

Where your data is kept

  • On your device: your songs, setlists, settings, and the sign-in details below are stored in your browser (IndexedDB and local storage) so that Chordless works offline. Clearing the site's data in your browser, or "Clear Local Data" in Chordless, removes them.
  • In your Google Drive: the files in the "Chordless" folder. They belong to you and are governed by Google's terms and privacy policy.
  • Sign-in: when you sign in, a small server component of Chordless, running on Cloudflare Workers, exchanges Google's sign-in code for tokens. It encrypts Google's refresh token into a blob with a key only the server holds, and gives the blob back to your browser to keep. It stores nothing itself. Later it decrypts the blob only to get a new short-lived Google access token for you, or to change who can open your Chordless folder, when you ask it to.

Cloudflare, which hosts the app, handles the web requests and may keep standard server logs, such as your IP address, under its own privacy policy. Chordless does not use cookies.

Anonymous usage reports

So that problems can be found and fixed, Chordless sends short reports of how it runs. They are on unless you turn them off.

  • What is in them: how long a sync or a sign-in refresh took and whether it worked, how many calls it made to Google Drive and to Chordless's server and how many failed, which page you were on, whether you were online, the setlist and song you had open (their identifiers, dates and CCLI numbers), the browser and device type (whether it is a phone, tablet or computer, its screen and window size, whether it has a touch screen, and the browser's name and version), and any error that happened, with the error's own text. That text, and a web address, can include a fragment of a song's title or lyrics. No personally identifiable data is sent.
  • Who they are from: no name, email address or Google account. Each device has a random id, made for this purpose and kept in the browser, which links one device's reports together. If you give us that id (for example when you ask for help), we can find your reports (the Contact page adds it to your message); otherwise nobody can tell whose they are. When you are signed in, reports from your different devices may be linked to each other.
  • Where they go: from your browser to Chordless's server, which does not keep them but passes them to Honeycomb, a service for looking at how software runs (its European service), where they are kept for 60 days and then deleted.
  • From Chordless's server: when you use it (signing in, team spaces, sharing), the server also reports each request it handles to Honeycomb, for the same 60 days: the address requested (which can contain the identifier of a space or a share), the answer, how long it took, your approximate location (country, region and city, worked out from your IP address, which is not kept), your network, and your browser and device type. These are not covered by the switch below.
  • Turning them off: Preferences, "Usage reports". The page then sends nothing, and the id for your device is deleted.

Sharing

We do not sell your data or give it to anyone. Information from Google is used only to provide Chordless's features described above. Chordless's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. It is not used to serve ads, and nobody reads it for any other purpose.

Emails you forward to Chordless

If you forward a worship leader's setlist email to Chordless's import address, it is read once, in memory, to find the songs: their titles, keys and CCLI numbers (from the attached PDFs), the date, and the name and email address of the person who sent it. Chordless replies to you with a link that holds only those details. The email and its PDFs are not stored or kept, nobody reads them, and the details in the link are read by your own device, not sent to us. Other people's addresses on the email (the To and Cc lists) are ignored.

Your choices

  • Sign out of Chordless, or clear its data, in the app.
  • Turn off the usage reports in Preferences.
  • Revoke Chordless's access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete the "Chordless" folder in Google Drive to delete the data it holds there.

Children

Chordless is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their information.

Changes and contact
